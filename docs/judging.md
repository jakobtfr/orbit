# Judging & Awards

Source: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/
Supplemented by: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/
Captured: 2026-11-14 (guide published 2026-11-13)

NASA Space Apps is framed as collaborative, not competitive — but projects that opt in are judged in three phases against five criteria.

## The five criteria

Every project is scored **1–5 on each criterion**, at every level of judging. A 5 is described as outstanding / groundbreaking / fully feasible / deeply relevant / exceptional.

| Criterion | What judges look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Impact** | Does it address a minor or major real problem? Quality and quantity of effect; breadth of audience helped or inspired. A 5 addresses a significant problem with extensive reach, potentially changing the world. |
| **Creativity** | Originality and innovation. A novel concept never attempted before, or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas. A 5 is a groundbreaking approach that challenges conventional methods. |
| **Validity** | Grounded in scientific principles; technically, functionally, or logically feasible; usable and user-friendly. A 5 is scientifically sound, fully developed, and ready for real-world implementation. |
| **Relevance** | Directly and fully answers the selected challenge, and **integrates NASA data as a core component** rather than a superficial addition. A 5 makes NASA data integral to the solution's success. |
| **Presentation** | Clarity of the challenge, solution, and significance; engaging, well-structured storytelling. Judges evaluate **both the demo and the full project** for this score. |

The guide explicitly warns that teams often polish the first four criteria and neglect Presentation. It recommends recruiting storytelling and design skills and devoting real time to the pitch.

## Three judging phases

| Phase | Who judges | What is selected | Announced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Local / Universal Event** | Local judges recruited and organized by the Local Lead; for the Universal Event, a team of subject-matter experts | **Global Nominees** | December 3, 2026 |
| **2. Global judging** | NASA and Space Agency Partner expert judges | **Global Finalists** and **Honorable Mentions** | December 17, 2026 |
| **3. Executive judging** | NASA and partner-agency leaders/executives | **10 Global Award winners** | January 26, 2027 |

Notes:

- Local awards are optional and **separate** from global advancement. Winning locally does not guarantee a Global Nomination.
- Only officially submitted projects are considered, regardless of local judging results.
- The number of Universal Event teams advancing is proportional to Local Event advancement.
- Honorable Mentions are ranked highly but do not advance to Executive Judging and are not eligible for awards.
- Global Winners are invited to a virtual Winner's Celebration in early 2027.

| Award | Given to |

| Best Use of Science | Best and most valid use of the scientific method |
| Best Use of Data | Best makes space data accessible or leverages it for a unique application |
| Best Use of Technology | Most innovative use of technology |

| Best Mission Concept | Most plausible concept and design |

| Best Storytelling | Most creatively communicates the potential of open data through storytelling |

| Art & Technology | Most effectively combines technical and creative skills |
| Local Impact | Greatest potential to make a difference in the local community |

## Eligibility gates for Global Judging

A team or project must meet all of these:

- Respond to one of the official 2026 challenges.
- All team members registered for the same Local Event (not waitlisted) or the Universal Event, and listed on the Members tab. Local Event teams need ≥1 person; Universal Event teams need ≥2. Maximum 6 members.
- Complete all required fields on the Project Submission page.
- Provide a demo: **≤30-second video or ≤7-slide deck** (title and intro slides included). Exceeding either limit makes the project ineligible for Global Judging.
- Use English on the submission page and in all linked materials. A demo video may be narrated in another language but must have English subtitles.
- Submit by **Sunday, November 15, 2026, 11:59 p.m. local time** for the team's Local Event (Universal Event: local time of the Team Owner).
- Certify original work and list **all** external resources — including open-source code, text, and images — in References. No copyrighted material without permission.
- Pass NASA's China funding-restriction review.

To be eligible for a Global Award, the project must use at least one NASA data source or resource.

## Implications for this project

| Criterion | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Relevance** | Make NASA data integral, not decorative. Use HRP risk/hazard data, OCHMO standards, or NASA open science data as inputs to the assessment logic itself. |
| **Validity** | Cite clinical thresholds and their confidence levels (see [`clinical-thresholds.md`](clinical-thresholds.md)); be explicit about what is prototype-grade vs. operational. |
| **Impact** | Lead with the deep-space / communication-delay scenario, and note the Earth analogue (remote communities with limited care access). |
| **Creativity** | Differentiate from a generic dashboard — e.g. the clickable body map, guided measurement coaching, or a crew-routine/gamification loop. |
| **Presentation** | Budget hours for the 30-second video or 7-slide deck. It counts, and an over-limit demo disqualifies from global judging. |
